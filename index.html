Research Interests

My core research goal is to advance AI Safety and Trustworthy AI, ensuring complex models remain reliable, transparent, and aligned. I approach these safety challenges primarily through the lens of Explainable AI (XAI) and Mechanistic Interpretability—using representation analysis and attribution frameworks as technical mechanisms to audit model behavior and verify safety guardrails.

About

I'm an undergraduate student in Software Engineering at the University of Dhaka (CGPA 3.86/4.00), working at the intersection of machine learning and interpretability. The black-box nature of modern deep learning models, and the stakes of deploying them in domains like healthcare and autonomous systems, is what drew me to Explainable AI. I'm still early in my journey, and my goal right now is to learn how to do quality, impactful research, and to help make neural networks' decision-making legible enough to trust in high-stakes settings.

I'm currently a research student at the Computational Intelligence and Operations Laboratory (CIOL), where I'm learning how to think, read, and work like a researcher under the guidance of my seniors and mentors. Alongside this, I've worked as an ML/Generative AI intern building production computer-vision systems, and as a software engineer on full-stack products. I'm now working to extend interpretability principles from smaller architectures (MLPs, CNNs) toward large language models, en route to a PhD.

Research

Explainable AI (XAI) AI Safety Mechanistic Interpretability World Models Machine Learning Deep Learning

My current work develops an interpretability framework for explaining the internal decision-making of deep architectures such as MLPs and CNNs, in collaboration with a faculty advisor and a peer researcher at the University of Dhaka. In parallel, I study mechanistic interpretability to build toward extending these transparency principles to large language models.
